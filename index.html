What is fixed and how the map is built

The engine is a line-for-line port of the reference TEA's cash flow (ProFAST method), fed by a parametric schedule calibrated to its 1 km and 2 km free-gas cases. Break-even price is cost-side NPV divided by revenue-side NPV per $/kg. Revenue scales exactly with q₀, and cost scales as a + b·q₀0.6 + c·q₀ (surface plant and its O&M on nameplate0.6, utilities per kg), so each cell is solved in closed form from three evaluations.

  • Financial basis at reference defaults: 10.2% real after-tax ROE, D/E 0.62, 4.4% real interest, 25.7% tax, 7-yr MACRS, 3 months cash on hand, 14.5% royalty, 100% availability, unsubsidized, 2026 dollars.
  • Timing as in the reference TEA: pre-discovery spend in years 0 to 2, producers drilled in year 3, surface plant in year 4, 30 years of production from year 5.
  • Production per producer follows Arps hyperbolic decline from q₀ (initial wellhead rate). Sold H₂ is 0.8955 × wellhead, a factor fitted to the reference production schedule (it reproduces that profile with Dᵢ = 0.05, b = 0.2 to within 0.05%). I read it as PSA recovery plus plant losses.
  • All pre-discovery spend is divided by chance of success, as in the reference TEA. Leasing/contractor/approval ($185k per producer at 600 m) and 2D seismic scale with field area = N × spacing². Drilling permits scale with N. Desk study, aerial survey and well-site surveys are fixed. Exploration drills 3 wells at producer cost plus $0.75M mobilization. The area scaling is my assumption, not something the reference TEA states.
  • Producer cost = $20k + $750k × depth(km)β. β = 1 reproduces the reference 2 km case. A power-law fit to oil and gas well costs gives β ≈ 1.6.
  • Surface plant = ($6.86M + $2.16M per km of depth) × (nameplate / 148.7 t/d)0.6. Maintenance, property tax, environmental and the surface part of G&A are the reference TEA's fractions of surface capital. Labor ($1.98M/yr) and 65% G&A on labor are fixed regardless of field size, as in the reference TEA.
  • Resource check: required φ·Sg·h = 30-yr wellhead cumulative ÷ (spacing² × ρH₂ × recovery factor). ρH₂ uses 10 MPa/km hydrostatic pressure × the overpressure ratio, 15 °C surface temperature plus the gradient, and Z from a second virial coefficient of 15 cm³/mol. It is a lower bound because production continues past year 30.
  • Delivered cutoff subtracts a constant adder. Delivery cost actually depends on throughput, distance and wellhead pressure. Published estimates span roughly $0.10 to $4/kg across those (Yang and Ogden, UC Davis).

Not modeled: raw-gas purity and its effect on PSA recovery and surface cost, helium or other by-product credits, compression for delivery as a function of depth, infill interference, and lead-time changes.
